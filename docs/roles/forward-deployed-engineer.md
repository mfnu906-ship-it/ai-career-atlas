# Forward deployed engineer

**Also seen as:** forward deployed AI engineer, forward deployed software engineer, deployment engineer, AI deployment engineer. **Center of gravity:** taking AI systems into real operating environments, often alongside customers.

## What the work can look like

The role commonly spans technical discovery, workflow mapping, system design, prototypes, integrations, evaluations, production deployment, adoption, and turning field lessons into reusable product capabilities. The balance changes: one employer may emphasize hands-on software engineering; another may include more solution design, customer enablement, or travel.

OpenAI describes its FDE work as embedding with teams, understanding workflows, building and evaluating solutions, integrating with existing systems, and helping bring them into production. A current healthcare FDE posting adds end-to-end ownership from discovery through production and adoption in a regulated setting. These are examples of one employer’s framing, not a universal standard. ## Common responsibilities

- Turn an ambiguous operational problem into a bounded technical project and measurable outcome.
- Build working AI applications, agentic workflows, APIs, integrations, and evaluation loops.
- Connect to customer data, identity systems, APIs, and operational software; handle permissions and reliability.
- Test with representative tasks and difficult exceptions; add monitoring, guardrails, and human review.
- Work directly with users and technical teams, then communicate patterns back to product and research teams.

## Skills employers may look for

Strong software engineering fundamentals; practical API and data integration; rapid prototyping; debugging unfamiliar environments; model and prompt behavior evaluation; system design; clear technical communication; comfort with ambiguity. Domain knowledge is especially useful when deployments involve healthcare, finance, government, law, or other constrained environments.

## Questions to ask about a posting

1. How much time is spent writing production code versus discovery, demos, or account support?
2. Who owns operations after go-live, and what does a handoff look like?
3. What are the travel, location, and customer-site expectations?
4. How is success measured: shipped systems, user adoption, business outcomes, reusable product capabilities, or some mix?
5. What controls exist for data access, reliability, privacy, and human review?

## Is it a fit?

This path may suit someone who likes building and debugging with users nearby, switching between software and domain problems, and seeing work reach production quickly. It may be less appealing if you prefer long stretches of isolated research or a narrowly scoped backend specialty.
