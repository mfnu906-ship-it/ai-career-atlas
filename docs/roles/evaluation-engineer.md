# AI evaluation engineer

**Also seen as:** AI evals engineer, model evaluation engineer, AI quality engineer, safety evaluation engineer. **Center of gravity:** making AI quality observable enough to guide development and release decisions.

## What the work can look like

Evaluation work turns an open-ended question—“does this system work well?”—into tasks, evidence, and decision rules. The engineer may design representative and adversarial datasets, build automatic and human grading pipelines, analyze failures, compare model or prompt changes, and set release gates. At some companies, this work sits inside product engineering, applied research, safety, or data science rather than under a dedicated title.

## Common responsibilities

- Translate user needs and risks into measurable evaluation criteria.
- Curate test cases that represent normal use, edge cases, and known failure modes.
- Build repeatable experiments and scoring tools; validate automated graders.
- Review disagreements between metrics, human judgment, and real-world outcomes.
- Work with model, product, and safety teams to turn findings into changes.

## Skills employers may look for

Experimental design, statistics, scripting and data analysis, dataset quality, software tooling, careful error categorization, and the ability to explain uncertainty. Domain expertise can matter when an evaluation depends on expert judgment.

## Questions to ask about a posting

1. Does the team evaluate model capability, product quality, safety risks, or all three?
2. How are benchmarks created, refreshed, and protected from leakage?
3. Which decisions depend on the evaluations, and who makes them?
4. How do automated graders get validated against human review?

## Is it a fit?

This path may suit people who enjoy turning ambiguous quality questions into careful experiments. It rewards skepticism: one score rarely captures whether an AI system is reliable, useful, or safe in context.
