# AI engineer

**Also seen as:** applied AI engineer, LLM engineer, agent engineer, AI product engineer. **Center of gravity:** building software products that use models as one part of a broader system.

## What the work can look like

The title is broad. In one organization it may mean integrating commercial foundation models into a user-facing application; elsewhere it may include model fine-tuning, retrieval systems, inference optimization, agent orchestration, or internal AI tooling. The job description should clarify whether the role is primarily product engineering, ML research and modeling, or infrastructure.

A typical product-oriented role may involve designing APIs and workflows around models, connecting data sources, building user experiences, measuring quality, managing latency and cost, and improving a system after release. AI features still need ordinary engineering: versioning, security, observability, fallbacks, and clear product behavior.

## Skills that travel across titles

- Software design and strong coding fundamentals
- Model APIs, structured outputs, tool use, and retrieval patterns
- Evaluation and testing for nondeterministic behavior
- Data handling, privacy, security, and production operations
- Product judgment: choosing when AI helps and when a simpler approach is better

## Questions to ask about a posting

- Is the work model training, application development, platform infrastructure, or a mix?
- Who owns evaluation and production incidents?
- Which models and data sources are in scope, and how often do they change?
- How does the team trade off quality, latency, cost, and user control?

## Is it a fit?

This path may suit software engineers who want to build model-powered products and are comfortable iterating where outputs are probabilistic. If the role emphasizes distributed serving, accelerators, or shared inference systems, compare it with AI platform or ML infrastructure roles.
