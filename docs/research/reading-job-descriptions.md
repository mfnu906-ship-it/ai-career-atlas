# How to read an AI job description

AI titles are changing faster than occupational categories. Read a posting as a set of signals and verify the details on the employer’s current careers page.

## 1. Find the work verbs

Highlight verbs such as **build**, **train**, **integrate**, **deploy**, **evaluate**, **operate**, **advise**, and **research**. These often reveal the role better than its title.

## 2. Identify the system boundary

Will you work on a model, an API or platform, an end-user product, a customer environment, or a research prototype? Look for named systems, data sources, users, and operational constraints.

## 3. Separate must-haves from wish lists

A long tool list can reflect one team’s current stack. Look for durable evidence: shipped systems, coding depth, data reasoning, reliability, experimentation, communication, or domain experience.

## 4. Locate ownership

Does the role own discovery, implementation, launch, monitoring, and improvement—or only one stage? Who handles support, incidents, evaluation, and decisions about release?

## 5. Check the practical conditions

Confirm seniority, location, work authorization, travel, on-call duties, compensation, and the amount of customer interaction. These details can change what an otherwise similar role feels like.

## Keep a comparison note

For each posting, record the date checked, exact title, company and domain, core outcomes, key tools, experience requirements, location and travel, and unanswered questions. Link to the original posting rather than copying large blocks of text. Job openings can expire; keep the observation date visible.

## Sources and method

The role summaries in this site are synthesized descriptions of recurring work patterns, not formal occupational definitions. Examples link to employer career pages so readers can inspect the original context.
